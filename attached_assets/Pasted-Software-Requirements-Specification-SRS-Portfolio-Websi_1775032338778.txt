Software Requirements Specification (SRS)
Portfolio Website Chatbot for Employer Interaction
1. Introduction
1.1 Purpose

This document defines the requirements for an interactive chatbot embedded in Farhan’s portfolio website. The chatbot is intended to assist real-time employers, recruiters, and visitors by answering questions about Farhan’s background, including his skills, projects, education, experience, academics, contact details, and portfolio content.

1.2 Problem Statement

The current chatbot appears to respond only to a narrow set of trigger words such as “Education” or “Contact.” It fails to handle natural human conversation, paraphrased questions, mixed sentences, and broader employer-style queries. This creates a poor user experience and may negatively affect employer perception of the portfolio.

1.3 Objective

The chatbot must support both:

direct keyword-based queries, and
full natural language questions or sentences.

It should respond intelligently, conversationally, and professionally to employer questions without repeatedly rejecting valid requests.

2. Analysis of Current Chat Issues

Based on the provided chat, the following major issues were identified:

2.1 Overdependence on Exact Trigger Words

The chatbot only responds properly to very specific words like:

“Education”
“Contact”

But it fails on similar valid phrases such as:

“Farhan’s academics”
“tell how good of a fresher in job sector is he”
“describe all of them please”
“This portfolio”
Impact

Real employers usually type full questions, not only menu keywords. This makes the chatbot feel rigid and unintelligent.

2.2 Failure to Understand Synonyms and Related Intents

The chatbot does not understand that the following may refer to the same domain:

academics = education
fresher quality = employability/profile summary
describe all of them = overview request
portfolio = website/project overview
Impact

The chatbot misses obvious intent and fails to provide helpful responses.

2.3 Repetitive Fallback Response

The bot repeatedly says:

“I'm here specifically to help with questions about Farhan's professional background. Could you ask me something about his skills, projects, education, or experience?”

This response is repeated even when the user already asked relevant questions.

Impact

This makes the chatbot feel broken, dismissive, and frustrating.

2.4 No Intent Recovery

When the question is unclear or broad, the chatbot does not attempt to:

interpret the likely intent,
give a summary,
provide options,
or ask a smarter clarifying question.

Example:

“describe all of them please”
“Okay skills then”
“Projects”

These should have been answerable immediately.

Impact

The chatbot forces the user to guess the “correct input format.”

2.5 Missing Conversational Context Handling

The chatbot does not use conversation history properly.

Example:

User says “describe all of them please” after a previous question.
User says “Okay skills then.”
User says “Projects.”

The bot should understand these as follow-up requests.

Impact

The conversation feels disconnected and robotic.

2.6 No Employer-Focused Summary Capability

When asked, “how good of a fresher in job sector is he,” the chatbot should provide a concise professional summary of Farhan as a fresher candidate.

Instead, it rejects the question.

Impact

This is a major failure, because employers often ask evaluative or summary-style questions.

2.7 Inconsistent Coverage of Supported Topics

The bot answers for:

education
contact

But fails for:

skills
projects
academics
portfolio overview
Impact

The chatbot appears incomplete and unreliable.

3. Corrected Interpretation of the Chat

Below is how the chatbot should have responded more professionally.

User:

“tell how good of a fresher in job sector is he”

Better response:

Farhan appears to be a promising fresher for entry-level software and technology roles. He is currently pursuing a B.Sc. in Software Engineering, has an academic foundation in mathematics and physics, and is building a portfolio that highlights his technical growth. As a fresher, his strengths would be learning ability, technical curiosity, and developing project experience. Employers looking for a junior candidate with potential may find him suitable, especially for software development, web development, or AI-assisted application roles.

User:

“describe all of them please”

Better response:

Certainly. Here is a quick overview of Farhan’s profile:

Skills: Technical and software-related competencies demonstrated through his portfolio and academic work.
Projects: Practical work that reflects his ability to apply knowledge in real scenarios.
Education: Currently pursuing a B.Sc. in Software Engineering at Daffodil International University.
Experience: Academic and project-based experience relevant to entry-level opportunities.

You can also ask me to explain any one of these in more detail.

User:

“Okay skills then”

Better response:

Farhan’s skills include software engineering fundamentals, problem-solving ability, and practical project development. His portfolio is designed to demonstrate both his technical learning and his readiness for entry-level opportunities. If you want, I can also break his skills into programming, tools, and soft skills.

User:

“Projects”

Better response:

Farhan’s projects represent his hands-on learning and practical application of software engineering concepts. They help demonstrate his problem-solving approach, technical growth, and ability to build usable systems. I can summarize his featured projects individually if needed.

User:

“Farhans academics”

Better response:

Farhan’s academic background includes a B.Sc. in Software Engineering at Daffodil International University, along with Cambridge A Levels in Physics and Mathematics. His academic path reflects a foundation in analytical thinking and technical study.

User:

“This portfolio”

Better response:

This portfolio is a professional showcase of Farhan’s education, technical skills, projects, and contact information. It is designed to help employers quickly understand his profile, strengths, and readiness for entry-level opportunities.

4. System Scope

The chatbot shall:

answer employer questions naturally,
understand both keywords and full sentences,
provide concise and professional replies,
support follow-up questions,
summarize Farhan’s profile,
explain projects, education, academics, contact, and portfolio purpose.

The chatbot shall not:

give unrelated answers,
require exact trigger words only,
repeatedly reject valid queries,
break conversation continuity.
5. Functional Requirements
5.1 Intent Recognition

The chatbot must identify user intent from both exact keywords and natural language sentences.

Supported intent categories:
Skills
Projects
Education
Academics
Experience
Contact Information
Portfolio Overview
Fresher Employability Summary
General Profile Summary
5.2 Synonym and Phrase Mapping

The chatbot shall recognize alternative terms and map them correctly.

Examples:
academics → education
studies → education
fresher quality → employability summary
portfolio → website overview
tell me everything → full profile summary
background → profile summary
qualifications → education + skills
what can he do → skills / projects
5.3 Natural Language Query Handling

The chatbot must answer questions phrased in natural English, including:

“How suitable is he as a fresher?”
“Can you summarize his background?”
“What kind of projects has he done?”
“Tell me about his academics.”
“What is this portfolio about?”
5.4 Follow-Up Context Retention

The chatbot shall maintain short-term conversational context for follow-up messages such as:

“describe all of them”
“skills then”
“projects”
“what about education?”
“and contact?”
5.5 Smart Clarification

If a question is too vague, the chatbot shall ask a useful clarification instead of rejecting it.

Example:

Instead of:

“Could you ask me something about his skills, projects, education, or experience?”

Use:

“I can help with Farhan’s skills, projects, education, experience, or contact details. Which one would you like to know more about?”

5.6 Employer-Focused Tone

All responses shall be:

professional,
concise,
polite,
portfolio-appropriate,
suitable for recruiters and employers.
5.7 Summary Generation

The chatbot shall provide:

brief profile summaries,
fresher suitability summaries,
academic summaries,
overall professional introductions.
5.8 Contact Response

The chatbot shall provide contact information clearly and professionally when requested.

It should ideally format contact details neatly instead of dumping them as plain text.

6. Non-Functional Requirements
6.1 Usability

The chatbot should be easy for employers to use without needing special commands.

6.2 Response Quality

Responses should be grammatically correct, relevant, and human-like.

6.3 Reliability

The chatbot should successfully answer at least 90% of common employer questions within its supported knowledge base.

6.4 Performance

Average response time should remain fast enough for real-time interaction.

6.5 Professional Presentation

The chatbot must reflect positively on the portfolio owner’s professionalism.

7. Suggested Conversation Design Improvements
7.1 Replace Rigid Trigger Logic

Current behavior suggests hardcoded keyword matching.
This should be replaced with:

intent classification,
synonym matching,
fallback semantic matching,
context-aware follow-up handling.
7.2 Add Intent Confidence Strategy

If confidence is high, answer directly.
If confidence is medium, answer with a best-fit interpretation.
If confidence is low, ask a short clarification.

7.3 Add Employer-Oriented Prebuilt Answer Types

The chatbot should support:

“Why should we consider him?”
“What are his strengths as a fresher?”
“What has he built?”
“What is his academic background?”
“How can we contact him?”
7.4 Improve Fallback Message
Bad fallback:

“I’m here specifically to help…”

Better fallback:

“I can help with Farhan’s skills, projects, education, experience, portfolio overview, or contact details. You can ask naturally, for example: ‘What projects has he done?’ or ‘How suitable is he for an entry-level role?’”

8. Acceptance Criteria

The chatbot will be considered acceptable if it can correctly respond to all of the following:

“Tell me about Farhan’s education.”
“What are his academics?”
“How good is he as a fresher?”
“Describe his profile.”
“What skills does he have?”
“Tell me about his projects.”
“What is this portfolio about?”
“How can I contact him?”
“Summarize everything briefly.”
Follow-up prompts like “skills then,” “projects,” and “what about education?”
9. Recommended Fixes Summary
Critical fixes
Add natural language understanding
Add synonym mapping
Add follow-up context handling
Stop repeating rejection messages
Add profile summary responses
Add portfolio overview response
Add fresher employability summary
Content fixes
Use professional recruiter-friendly wording
Give concise but informative answers
Structure contact information clearly
Support broad questions, not just menu terms
10. Final Recommendation

For a portfolio site viewed by real employers, this chatbot should behave like a professional assistant, not like a keyword filter. It must understand human language naturally, answer broad profile questions, and guide employers smoothly through Farhan’s qualifications.

Right now, the chatbot gives the impression that the system is incomplete. Fixing the issues above will make it more reliable, more professional, and more useful in real hiring contexts.
